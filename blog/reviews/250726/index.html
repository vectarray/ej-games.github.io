Welcome to the FOURTH technology review from Vectarray, featuring the laptop that I am currently using as my main device!!
What an exciting review, for basically just me!

This laptop is very versatile, and reasonably powerful, but being a 360° hinge laptop, it comes with a catch.

On the way out
This model is phasing out, and replaced by the (arguably better) Book5 Pro 360.

Overview


This is a very worthy laptop - it folds, it has an Intel Ultra 7 "under the hood", and offers fairly decent battery life, with a great OLED screen. But, it does have its drawbacks.
  • Powerful CPU
  • Built-in Windows AI features with an NPU
  • Lovely 3K 120 Hz screen
  • Good battery life for average tasks
  • GPU is underpowered for heavy tasks
  • No media keys apart from volume
  • Unimpressive battery life when in medium-heavy use

Performance

The Galaxy Book4 Pro 360 has the following options for hardware:

CPU:
CPU Option Equivalent Desktop CPU Equivalent Apple CPU
Core Ultra 5 125H Core i3 14100 Apple M2
Core Ultra 5 135H Core i5 14400 Apple M2 Pro
Core Ultra 7 155H Apple M3
Core Ultra 7 165H

Memory: 16 GB or 32 GB.

Storage: 256 GB, 512 GB, or 1 TB.

Graphics: All have Intel Arc graphics, which are just integrated Intel GPUs.
Overall, the CPU is very capable at virtually every task you can throw at it; the memory is extremely quick at 7467 MHz; the storage is... well, storage; the GPU is quite underpowered - it would have been much nicer to have a dedicated GPU for stuff like photo and video editing.

The gaming experience on this laptop is not amazing at all, thanks to the underpowered GPU. It can barely run Minecraft without reaching 100% GPU and getting very hot.

But I should stress that the integrated GPU is perfectly powerful for general use. It can easy handle 1 external monitor, and could probably handle 2 with minimal problems.

Display and Input

The Book4 Pro series from Samsung all have a 2880x1800 120 Hz AMOLED screen, which at least for this model, is very impressive.

This resolution at 16" gives about 212 pixels per inch.
This much greater than any 1080p laptop of a similar size (~140 ppi).

The keyboard is a slim-style keyboard with not much height to the keys - I personally prefer this type of keyboard, but some people hate it - use this information how you like.

One definite downside to this keyboard, though, is the lack of media keys, so if you want to control music playing or something, you have to rely on either the alien buttons on your headphones, or navigate to your music app and manually change it. Oh my god! May as well forget this laptop then.

The trackpad is also very large, as you can see. In my opinion, this is fine, because what else are you going to use that space for?
But this also means that when you are using both hands on the trackpad, you have to put your "clicking" finger right in the corner in order for it to register.

Practicality

In my opinion, 16" is the largest I would be happy with. It's perfectly large enough to fit as much on the screen without taking up an unholy amount of desk space, like some of those 19" laptops you can find.

The 16"-ness of this laptop also has an unwanted side effect, mainly that it is quite heavy, at around 1.66 kg (3.66 lb), but given the power and capability of this laptop, it's worth it - it would be hard to find a folding laptop with this many features that weighs less.

Tablet Mode & S Pen

What!! A 360° laptop has a tablet mode? Shocker...

The laptop works fine in every configuration, apart from one: the "almost" tablet mode, shown on the left.

In this position, every time you touch the screen, either with the pen or with your fat finger, it wobbles profusely for up to 10 seconds. "Oh, here, look at this image I just made", said John, who then proceeded to show a completely unintelligible oscillating photo.

It also likes to be pushed down into the "proper" tablet mode if you press slightly too hard, and can sometimes gradually float down by itself and eventually crash down with a fairly startling sound.
In the full "tablet" mode, with the screen parallel to the main body, while there is no problem with being pushed down, there are two main quirks:
  1. The air vents on the main body are blocked by the back of the screen, causing the laptop to heat up and blow hot air into your hands and arms.
  2. The magnets (which keep the screen and body together when the laptop is closed) interfere with the S Pen, causing it to be off by up to a centimetre around the edges of the laptop. This is obviously easy to work around by just avoiding the edges.
Colourful section to catch your eye
Thanks, this is just a colourful box to catch your eye, and tell you: these are all fairly minor quirks.

The S-Pen is fairly good at most tasks, and is very pleasant to write with. It also has no battery, and doesn't need to be recharged, but this also means the button on the pen only works if you move the pen far away then back towards the screen.

Battery Life

The battery life for this laptop is both poor and good.

When used lightly, like writing on a Word document, or casually browsing the internet, it will last about 6-7 hours on a single charge, at least from 80% from my experience - I have battery health mode set to stop charging at 80%.

When used heavily, like in high performance mode, video editing, or anything that would cause the laptop to heat up, it would probably last 3-4 hours on a single charge (from 80%).

Bear in mind that writing on the laptop in tablet mode is effectively "heavy use" - the laptop gets hot as the vents are blocked, and for some reason, the Samsung system software automatically enters high performance mode in tablet mode, which means the battery when actively writing is limited.*

*This may be just a problem with my laptop, or my workflow.

Charging
The laptop box comes with a 65 watt USB-C adaptor, and a two-ended USB-C cable to charge using one of the USB-C ports on the laptop.

Reliability

The laptop is fairly reliable so far, fingers crossed, etc. - although I have had it for just under a year.

There are 3 main problems I have run into:
Random Throttling
This is very likely to be a software issue in something I have installed, and only happens very occasionally - so it's not a massive deal either way.

Every so often, the laptop takes a couple of minutes to turn on, then when it does turn on, is painstakingly slow to do anything. I have tried everything to fix it, and all I know is that the CPU is being temperature-throttled, likely because the fans don't switch on, or something. I don't know.
Screen Hinges
The hinges on a 360° screen are bound to weaken and wear over time, but it's still worth mentioning. At some places around the edges of the screen, especially near the hinges, there are creaking sounds when you touch them. Nothing more than a mild annoyance.
Bottom of the laptop
After a few months of use, the bottom pads on the laptop had worn away fairly significantly, and the bottom of the laptop had slightly bowed (nowhere near the battery, don't worry). This would be fine, if it wasn't bowing right at the air intake vents. Which means air struggles to get into the laptop to cool it.

This is easily fixed by putting something on the bottom of the laptop to raise it up, so again, not much more than a mild annoyance.

Built-in Software

There are three noteworthy apps that come installed with the laptop:
Samsung Settings
While it's quite klunky, it works well enough. This app controls the power/fan mode (i.e. quiet, normal, high performance), and also controls the HDR display settings.

However, it also clashes with the built-in windows HDR settings - only one can be on at once. The Samsung HDR is more vibrant in normal use, but as soon as you watch a real HDR video, the HDR doesn't really work. The Windows HDR shows the normal display colours in normal use, but supports HDR video, so bright areas of a video will actually be brighter than the entire rest of the screen.
Quick Share
If you've got another Samsung device, or even Android device, then you'll already be familiar with Quick Share - it's effectively Samsung's rival to Apple AirDrop.

The only benefit of Quick Share over AirDrop is that Quick Share works with every Android device, not just Samsung ones.
Samsung Notes
The Samsung Notes app for PC works fairly seamlessly with syncing notes, and is a pretty good way of jotting something down on your phone and coming back to it on the laptop.

The only problem is that quite a few of the Samsung Notes mobile features aren't available on PC, and the app for PC is pretty bare-bones.

Conclusion

I don't really know how to rank laptops on a scale from 1 to 10 yet, as I've only ever owned two.

But by all means, buy this laptop if you want. If you don't want to buy it, you still have to. By reading the previous sentence, you have entered a legally binding contract with Samsung.
