Welcome to the FIFTH technology review from Vectarray, where I will be reviewing the Samsung Galaxy S21 FE, the phone that I used second-most recently! I believe I had the perfect grounds to get rid of this phone, which means you're in for a lot of constructive criticism of how it performs in daily life!

I owned this phone for just over a year before I chose to get rid of it. I bought it new.

Technical Specs


This amazing smart mobile telephone features:
  • Snapdragon 888 CPU (8 cores, ~2.8 GHz)
  • 120 Hz 6.4" screen
  • 32 MP photo camera
  • 4K video at 60fps
  • 13 hours battery life*
*If you're browsing the web the entire day. But I don't believe this figure anyway! We'll get onto that.

My Experience

This phone, at least the second edition of it that I own, contains the Snapdragon 888 processor. If you're from the US, Canada, China, Taiwan, Hong Kong or Japan (what a list), then that's the same processor as the rest of the Galaxy S21 series. Which released in early 2021.

The key thing to note about this is that the S21 FE released a year later in January 2022, then didn't release an S22 FE, which meant that the S21 FE remained on the market for far too long. I bought the phone in October 2023. Which means this brand new phone contained a processor which was about two and a half years old. My flagship S21 FE phone, using a 2021 processor, was competing with the S23, using a 2023 processor.

But worry not! We all know the best way to improve performance is, of course, to wait for the phone to get quicker! Unfortunately, in this isolated case, that only made the phone slower. By January 2025, i.e. having owned the phone for just over a year, the phone's processor was practically 4 years old. Opening the camera crashed the phone, and searching in Settings took about a minute. This was a phone I bought new, less than a year ago.

Needless to say, I upgraded to an S25 in January 2025, near enough launch day.

Performance

Depending on who you are, what you look like, and most importantly, where you live, you'll get one of two processor, either of:
  • Samsung Exynos 2100
    • US, Canada, China, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Japan
  • Snapdragon 888
    • Literally everywhere else
Exciting stuff!

In my experience, the phone was decently snappy, until software updates basically killed the performance. According to one of those silly old benchmark sites, both processors are basically identical, but the Snapdragon is marginally better at gaming.

Connectivity

Camera Quality

On the back, the S21 FE sports a 12MP main camera (1x), a 12MP ultrawide camera (0.5x), and an 8MP telephoto camera (3x). But more interestingly, because big Samsung know exactly what we humans do all the time, the selfie camera supports up to 32 Megapixels. Personally, I don't think they've fit a 32MP sensor into the camera, and the rest of it is done by upscaling the original lower-resolution image... but most people say it is a 32MP sensor.

Video Quality

This phone supports up to 60fps of 4K video! Which would be good if the processor could handle it...

Alternatively, you can do 720p@30fps, 1080p@30/60fps, and if I remember correctly, QHD (1440p) is also an option.

Ease of Use

This phone is about as easy to use as any other Samsung phone. Performance does get quite bad depending on how many apps you have open, and the battery life is pretty bad as well... I mean the figures claim 13 hours of web browsing, but I could never really get more than 3-4 hours of continued use out of this phone.

Being a Samsung phone, all of the gestures are there, as well as the navigation bar for all of you actual psychopaths.

Other than that, it was a fairly standard phone experience. OneUI does get a bit annoying when you do certain things like, you know, want to search for an app (it took soooo long) but that's fairly minor I guess?

Conclusion

I can only imagine this was a solid phone when it came out. But now it's got such an old processor that this phone won't beat the competition.

For example, as of writing, you can pick up an S21 FE for about £130 second hand in mediocre condition. For £170, you can get an S23! Much better processor, etc etc.

Big picture: not really viable unless you're strapped for cash.
